# Stone and basic gem models

Open [the standalone preview](preview/index.html) in a WebGL-capable browser. It contains the renderer and models and needs no network connection.

Use **Replay stone merge** or the **Merge** slider to inspect the wall transition. **New stone variation** changes the boulder silhouette and stone shade. **Inspect** opens each gem at close range. The view controls switch between isometric and top views, rotate by 90 degrees, and remove colour. A creep walks the lane behind the tower rows and every tower aims its head at the nearest creep; **Pause target** stops the walk, and reduced-motion mode starts it paused.

This revision replaces the earlier voxel wall style and raises the gems on architectural stone towers. Each gem type has its own tower shape, towers join the walls they stand in, and a row shows the Diamond tower at the six quality tiers. Placeholder creeps walk a corridor at their VPK-derived scale.

These are real three.js meshes generated by [models.js](models.js). To build or redesign a tower, follow the [tower model spec](TOWER-SPEC.md). They are an initial model proposal for the game. They do not change gameplay or the accepted board geometry. No files in `src/` are changed.

## Models

- Stone is dark rock that forms continuous jagged ridges. Each cell has a craggy core; each cardinal neighbour adds a low ridge plus three leaning crags. Its height stays at or below 0.20 cell. A stable coordinate/seed hash changes the outline, crags and shade.
- Cardinal neighbours extend the stone to the shared cell boundary. Straights, corners, T junctions and crosses use the same generator. Diagonal cells do not join.
- Merge progress extends and raises the connecting ridges and crags over 1.1 seconds in the preview. The slider also supports reverse inspection. Reduced-motion mode switches between endpoints.
- Eight basic gem models stand on stone towers, each with a type-specific silhouette. Six use a lathe shaft that shares its gem's facet count: Topaz Y waisted (three prongs), Ruby R keep (eight merlons), Emerald G spire (four prongs), Opal E cup, Aquamarine Q stepped and Amethyst P drum.
- Diamond D is a square tower of stacked, bevelled stone courses on a battered foot. A pale stone corbel and dark slate claws hold a large brilliant cut, point down: a table, 16 crown facets, a girdle and a pavilion. Fixed ice tones per facet, from vertex colours, make the cut read under flat shading; per-face shade variation does the same for the masonry. Its white star-cut crown and dark claws keep it distinct in grayscale and in top view.
- Sapphire B is a straight, six-sided column of bevelled stone courses on a battered foot, with a wider pale capital. A dark socket and three grips (six from flawless) hold a large pointed blue hexagonal prism; the setting leans with the gem. Flawed adds six face inlays, normal a third course, flawless three more grips and perfect a lower collar band; huge only grows. Shaft and cap palette colours share one vertex-coloured masonry material. At huge it uses 21 meshes, 518 triangles and four materials including the plinth, with a top of 1.105 cells and a creep-visibility margin of 0.104 at rest / 0.096 aimed.
- A tower stands on its cell's stone: the same boulder, shade and joining ridges a wall would have at that cell. Pass the cell's `x`, `z`, `seed` and `neighbours`; `setMerge` animates its joins with the walls.
- `quality` 1–6 (chipped to huge) scales the tower above the stone base uniformly; the base stays wall-sized so its joins meet the cell edge. Widths are 0.38 / 0.44 / 0.50 / 0.56 / 0.62 / 0.68 cell; height caps are 0.68 / 0.78 / 0.87 / 0.97 / 1.06 / 1.16 cell. The Diamond also gains one detail per step: faceted ice lozenges on its top course from flawed, a third course at normal, eight claws instead of four at flawless and a fourth course at perfect; huge only grows. Its height stays below the cap (1.07 at huge) because a taller crown would hide the smallest creep's top.
- Each tower's head (its prongs or claws, and its gems) turns on the shaft axis toward the creep under attack. `userData.aimAt(x, z)` takes a world-space board point: it turns the head to face the point and tips the gems about 10° toward it, so even round gems show their aim. The Diamond's claws and stone tip together; cluster crystals each tip on their own base, so they stay seated. `userData.aimAt()` returns the head to rest. At rest every tower is unchanged.
- Gems use opaque flat shading. Gem profiles and crystal counts reinforce the type.
- `createCreep({ modelScale, flying, boss })` builds a boxy placeholder creep facing +x: light body, dark trim and visor. Body length is `creepSize(modelScale)` = 0.36 × ModelScale cell, so the original size ratios hold; height is 0.66 × length. Air units fly at `FLY_ALTITUDE` (1.35 cell) above a ground shadow. Bosses add shoulder armour. `userData.healthBarY` anchors the HUD health bar; the study shows bars, with the HP number on the boss. These proxies fix scale and readability; creep designs come later.

## Reuse in the game

```js
import { createStone, createGemTower } from "./models.js";

const wall = createStone({
  x: 4,
  z: 3,
  seed: 42,
  neighbours: { north: true, east: true },
});
scene.add(wall);
wall.userData.setMerge(0.5); // Presentation progress, independent of simulation ticks.

const emerald = createGemTower("G", {
  x: 5,
  z: 3,
  seed: 42,
  quality: 3, // Normal
  neighbours: { west: true },
});
scene.add(emerald);
emerald.userData.aimAt(creep.position.x, creep.position.z); // Each frame while it attacks; aimAt() rests it.
```

When adjacency changes, rebuild the affected stone cells with the same seed and animate their merge progress. The game owns neighbour lookup, timing and placement validity. It also owns targeting: pick the creep, then aim at its ground position (an air unit's shadow cell). `aimAt` turns the head immediately; to ease the turn, aim at an interpolated point. Geometry generation does not belong in `src/sim/`. P2 can adopt these factories into its owned rendering module after art review.

Each mesh owns its geometry. Stone materials are shared and marked with `userData.sharedMaterial`; keep them when removing a model. Gem materials belong to each tower. This study does not batch the entire board. Measure the full board before choosing that optimisation.

## Run and validate

Use the dependencies already installed for the foundation phase. On this art branch, install them from the foundation checkout if `node_modules` is absent; the art study adds no packages.

From the repository root:

```powershell
node node_modules/vite/bin/vite.js --host 127.0.0.1 --port 5175
node docs/art/model-study/models.test.mjs
```

Visit `/docs/art/model-study/index.html` on that server. Rebuild the standalone file after source changes:

```powershell
node --input-type=module -e 'import {build} from "vite"; import {viteSingleFile} from "vite-plugin-singlefile"; await build({root:"docs/art/model-study",configFile:false,plugins:[viteSingleFile()],build:{outDir:"preview",emptyOutDir:false}});'
```

The geometry check covers all 16 cardinal neighbour masks, merge bounds, edge joins, deterministic variation, distinct tower profiles, bounds for all eight types at all six tiers at rest and with the head aimed at eight compass points, heads that lean toward targets from an off-origin cell and rest again, Diamond and Sapphire detail steps per tier, Sapphire geometry/material budgets and visibility margins, tower bases that match and join their cell's stone, creep sizes for ModelScale 1.0–6.0 (ground creeps up to 5.0 clear corridor walls), health-bar anchors and invalid inputs. It also prints an occlusion probe: it casts rays from the isometric camera at four quarter-turns and one axis-aligned angle to the neighbouring cell centres at creep height. Each cell is checked with the head at rest and aimed at that cell, its worst lean. Chipped and flawed towers hide none. Normal to huge towers hide one, the cell diagonally behind. The check fails if any tower hides more. A second probe aims at the top of the smallest creep: no tower hides it in the four default views, and an axis-aligned view hides it behind one flawless-or-larger tower at most. Browser review covers playback, head tracking, seed changes, close-ups, both camera modes, quarter-turn rotation and grayscale. Saved screenshots capture the runnable meshes.

Creep designs and animation, special towers, combat effects, occluder fading, game integration and full-board performance remain outside this study. Small screens need **Inspect** to see gem detail. The geometric silhouettes are proposals; this study does not establish their readability during combat.
